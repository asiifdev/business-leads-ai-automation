# Features

What Prospex does today, what each part is for, and how far it has been verified. If something is not listed here as working, assume it does not exist yet.

**Status key**

| Mark | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Verified** | Run against the real thing (real Google Maps, real chat exports, real websites, a real database) during development. |
| **Simulated** | Tested end to end with a stand-in for an outside service (for example a fake WhatsApp). The logic is tested, the real service is not. |
| **Partial** | Works, with a limit that matters. |
| **Planned** | Not built. |

## 2. Find and rank prospects

| Feature | What it does and why | Where | Status |
|---------|----------------------|-------|--------|
| **Google Maps scraping** | Searches Google Maps for each query and area with a headless Chromium. Per business: name, address, phone, website, rating, review count, coordinates, **the real Maps category**. Progress is reported per search. Duplicates (same phone, or same name and address) are removed. A typed city is searched where you asked: the search is anchored on the city's coordinates and results farther than about 50 km are dropped. A campaign searches one country (chosen when it is made; default Indonesia): the place is looked up as "<place>, <country>", and outside Indonesia a text such as "Austin, Texas" is first checked as one place before it is split into several. If Maps cannot find a place the search is not anchored and the log says so. Several cities can be separated by commas, `;`, `/`, `&`, `+`, "dan" or new lines. "Seluruh Indonesia" becomes one search per city, biggest markets first, 40 or more results per search (Google lists about 120 at most), with random pauses; the campaign stops as soon as the target is reached. A campaign can ask for up to 2,000 leads. | Campaigns | Verified live for typed cities. A full 1,000-lead nationwide run has not been timed. Countries other than Indonesia: the area rules and the data passed to Maps are unit-tested with a stand-in Maps, and two real campaigns were run through the queue against live Google Maps for Egypt (cafés in "Cairo": 10 leads at Cairo's coordinates; pharmacies in "Giza, Egypt": 10 leads, addresses in Arabic and English, phones stored as +20 numbers). Not verified: other countries, a nationwide run, WhatsApp checks and drafts for non-Indonesian numbers, phones written in a page's running text. "Seluruh Indonesia" is still Indonesian only |
| **Lead sources** | A campaign chooses where to look: Google Maps, OpenStreetMap, or both (tried in the order chosen; a later source fills only what the earlier ones left of the target, and the same business found by two sources becomes one lead with the gaps filled and each field's source recorded). **OpenStreetMap** turns the place into a box with Nominatim, the search terms into tags (about 50 business types by their Indonesian and English names, or a tag typed as `shop=camera`; other terms are searched in business names) and asks Overpass once per place. It returns only named places that have a phone, website or email, with address, category and coordinates; it has no ratings, and coverage depends on volunteers' mapping. The data is © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL) and the lead page says so. A source that fails is shown as a note on the campaign while another still delivers; if nothing is found anywhere the campaign fails with the reason. | Campaigns (new campaign form, setup card), API (`sources` on a campaign) | Verified: a real OpenStreetMap run through the queue and worker (Bandung, public Nominatim and Overpass) and merging, failure and ordering with stand-in sources. The public Overpass took about 40 seconds for a small query from this machine; set `OVERPASS_URL` for your own. Not verified: other cities, nationwide runs, or more than the listed business types |
| **Overture Maps source** | Open place data (Meta, Foursquare, Microsoft and others, under CDLA-Permissive-2.0, Apache-2.0 or CC0) read with DuckDB straight from Overture's public files: no key, no cost. Business types map to Overture categories (about 50 in Indonesian and English, or `category=camera_store`; other terms are searched in names). Only places with a phone, website, email or social page and a confidence of at least 0.25 are returned, best first, mobile numbers preferred, closed places marked. It reaches small businesses that only have a Facebook page. The first search after the server starts takes several minutes (292 seconds in our test: reading the files' index), later ones seconds (1 second); `OVERTURE_PLACES_URL` points at a local copy. DuckDB is loaded only when used. | Campaigns (new campaign form), API (`sources: ["overture"]`) | Verified on the real public Overture files through DuckDB (`scripts/overture-check.ts`): a first search for restaurants in Bandung returned 10 places, all with a phone, in 292 seconds; a second search with other terms returned in 1 second. Not verified: other cities, a local copy, a nationwide run. Not verified in a Docker image (the image builds dependencies on Alpine and runs on Debian) |
| **Website list** | Paste up to 200 websites or Instagram/Facebook pages into a campaign; each is opened (safe fetcher) and the business name comes from the page's structured data, site name or title, with phone, email and address when the page states them. Pages that cannot be opened, are parked, or show a login wall are named in the campaign's note and left out; nothing is invented. Link pages and marketplaces are skipped. | Campaign page ("Import file" → Websites), API (`POST /scraper/campaigns/:id/import/websites`) | Verified with stand-in pages (names, details, parked, unreachable, login wall, concurrency) and end to end against a running API on real websites (`scripts/sources-check.ts`) |
| **File import** | Add businesses from a CSV (comma, semicolon or tab; UTF-8 or Windows-1252) or an .xlsx file to any campaign that is not running: up to 2,000 rows and 5 MB. The columns are found from their headings in English or Indonesian (name, phone, email, website, address, category, city, latitude, longitude), shown with a sample and what would happen, and can be corrected before importing. Values that do not look right (a phone that is not a number, an email without a domain, a website that is not a web address) are left out and counted; rows without a name are skipped; rows repeating each other are merged. Imported leads then go through the same steps as scraped ones: fit, score, repeats against all earlier leads, data-quality rating, contact enrichment. A second import adds to the campaign. | Campaign page ("Import file"), API (`POST /scraper/campaigns/:id/import/preview`, `POST /scraper/campaigns/:id/import`) | Verified against a running API, database and queue (semicolon CSV, preview, bad values, a repeat of an OpenStreetMap lead, a second import, a running campaign, another workspace). The .xlsx reader is unit-tested on hand-built files, not on files saved by Excel or LibreOffice. The dialog is type-checked and builds but was not clicked through in a browser |
| **No invented leads** | If scraping fails the campaign fails with a clear message. Prospex never fills the gap with made-up businesses. `SCRAPER_MODE=fixture` exists for tests and demos only: clearly labelled leads with no phone number. | Campaigns | Verified |
| **Lead score** | A 0 to 100 index from signals in the listing (reachable by phone, rating weighted by review count, review volume) plus an AI judgement of fit with your offer. It ranks leads, it does not predict sales. There are no hard-coded industry or city weights. | Leads | Verified |
| **Fit judgement** | For each business, the AI reads its name and real Maps category against your offer and brief, and says high, medium or low with a one-line reason. The name counts: "Rental Kamera" in a name matters even if the Maps category is generic. | Lead detail | Verified. Needs an AI provider; small local models can misjudge, which is why the reason is shown |
| **Map and list** | Leads on a map and in a table, filtered by priority and status. Lists are paged ("Page 1 of 17", the title shows the real total) and the map loads every lead with coordinates, not the first page. | Campaign detail, Leads | Verified in a browser |
| **Search and area** | One search box matches name, address, phone (so "0812" finds numbers stored as 62812), website, email, category and area. Province, city/regency and district are worked out from each lead's coordinates in the background (OpenStreetMap Nominatim: one request a second, nearby leads share one lookup) after a scrape or from the "Classify area" button, and filter the list and the map. Google Maps addresses rarely name the city, which is why the coordinates are used. | Leads | Verified: search, filters and the map in a browser; area lookup against the real service for a sample of leads. Running it over a whole workspace takes as long as the number of distinct places divided by one a second. |
| **Contact and business enrichment** | Reads what a lead publishes about itself. **With a website**: the home page plus up to two contact or about pages; it stores the emails, WhatsApp numbers and social profiles printed there, and the business details the site states about itself (description, type, opening hours, address, price range, from its schema.org data and meta tags). A `wa.me/<username>` link is opened and the number its public profile shows is recorded. Pages with almost no HTML text are opened in the browser. **Without a website**, or when the "website" is an Instagram or Facebook page, or when the source listed such a page (OpenStreetMap does), it reads that page's public preview (the name and bio that a chat app shows under a shared link: one request per page, no posts, no followers, no login) and takes the phone numbers and emails written in the bio. TikTok and link pages (Linktree) are not read. It only fills empty fields (a Maps phone, address or email is never replaced), a number from structured data counts only if it is a mobile number, addresses go through the same filter as printed ones (template placeholders such as info@example.com are dropped), and each filled field records its source. A page that shows a login wall is reported as unreachable, never guessed. The business details are given to the draft writer inside the untrusted-data block as background only. Runs automatically after a scrape, before bulk drafting when the reading is over 90 days old, on demand (lead page, campaign page) and workspace-wide with "Refresh old data" (up to 50 per press). | Lead detail, Campaign detail, Leads ("Refresh old data"), API (`POST /leads/:id/enrich`, `POST /leads/enrich/campaign/:id`, `POST /leads/enrich/stale`, 50 per call) | Verified against a real database with stand-in pages (website with structured data, contact page, placeholder email, Instagram and Facebook previews, a login wall, nothing to read, refresh of old readings, other workspaces untouched) and on the real public Instagram and Facebook pages of one large account (`scripts/enrichment-check.ts`, `LIVE=true`). Not verified: small local businesses' real pages, the browser fallback and the screens on a real client-rendered site. Instagram and Facebook can change or block the preview at any time. The fit judgement is made when leads are found, before any page is read, so it does not use these details |
| **Data quality** | Each lead records where its fields came from, the results of free checks (website live or parked, e-mail domain can receive mail, number is on WhatsApp), the listing's closed status, and whether it repeats an earlier lead (same phone, website domain, or same name at the same place). From these a lead is rated high, medium or low, with reasons. Bulk drafting skips low leads, numbers not on WhatsApp and dead e-mail domains before any AI call; medium problems show as flags on the draft. Data not checked for 90 days is flagged as stale. Nothing paid is used. | Lead detail, Outbox, API (`dataQuality`, `qualityIssues`, `validation` on every lead) | Verified against a real Postgres (migrations from scratch, `scripts/data-quality-check.ts`: repeats across campaigns and inside a batch, closed listings, filter, recompute, the WhatsApp and e-mail gates, real DNS). Simulated: the WhatsApp lookup uses a fake provider, so a real linked account has not been asked; the closed-listing text is tested on sample text, not on a live Maps card. Leads saved before this feature get their level and repeat marks from "Recheck data" (drafting applies the rules to them even before that). Single-lead drafts are flagged, not blocked. |
| **Export** | CSV, JSON, vCard. | Campaign detail | Existing feature |

## 3. Write in your own voice

| Feature | What it does and why | Where | Status |
|---------|----------------------|-------|--------|
| **AI Style** | Learns how you write from your own WhatsApp chat exports: reply length, emoji, how you open and close. Stores statistics and anonymized question and answer pairs, never the raw chats. Delete it any time. | AI Style | Verified on real exports |
| **Draft writer** | Writes first messages and follow-ups from the lead's real data, your style, the matching angle from your brief, and the facts in your knowledge base. An offer is read as a finished product (a demo and a trial make sense) or as a service (a first message introduces the company, names its whole range of work in plain words and invites a conversation, never offering a demo that does not exist). There is no character limit; checks in code reject a question without a question word, slang, jargon, a missing introduction and clients named in your brief, and the draft is written again with the reasons. A WhatsApp first message says who you are and what you offer in the first sentence (in a real test, hook-first messages without that were taken for customers asking to rent), then one relevant point, one concrete thing from the brief and one easy question, and carries an opt-out line. An email opens with "Halo,", ends with "Salam,", and can carry one link you set yourself (added by the system, never written by the model). Drafts only mention capabilities that fit the lead's kind of business, never guess an honorific (Bapak, Ibu, Mbak), and contain no markdown symbols. | Outbox | Verified with local models. gemma4 still sometimes interprets a rating or repeats stiff phrases, so read before approving |
| **Fit gate and relevance check** | A business the AI judged a poor fit (for example a camera shop that sells, offered a rental system) gets no draft; medium fits only when you tick "include medium fits". After a first message is written, words that belong to another kind of business in your brief ("kebaya" in a draft for a costume shop) trigger one more attempt, and if they remain the draft is flagged "other-business". Facts are searched with the lead's own kind of business first. | Outbox | Tested with real examples from this project; the AI's fit judgement itself is only as good as the model |
| **One message for many leads** | Write one message, or have the AI write one for a kind of business (a single call), then pick leads and create one draft each. Kinds come from the leads' Maps categories; a name search narrows further. `{nama}` and `{kategori}` are filled per lead. Leads without a contact, opted out, or already messaged are skipped and counted; a number or address is never drafted twice. WhatsApp drafts get the opt-out line, emails the footer. Drafts appear in the Review tab like any other. | Outbox → Review | Covered by tests with a stand-in database; not yet used on a real campaign |
| **Standard spelling** | Chat habits (all lowercase, no punctuation, "gk", "yg") are not copied. Capitals, punctuation, "Halo, Kak," and decimal commas follow PUEBI, enforced after the AI writes. | Outbox | Verified |
| **Risk flags** | A draft that contains links, prices, discounts or bank details that no source supports, reads meaning into a rating ("shows high trust"), guesses an honorific, or answers a customer message that reads like an instruction to the AI, is flagged for the reviewer. | Outbox | Verified |

## 4. Review, send, reply

| Feature | What it does and why | Where | Status |
|---------|----------------------|-------|--------|
| **Outbox** | Every message starts as a draft. You approve, edit or reject. Nothing is sent without approval. | Outbox | Verified |
| **WhatsApp connection** | Links a number as a device (Baileys) by QR code. Sessions survive restarts. | Outbox | Verified: pairing, resuming after restart, one real message sent and one real reply received. Not yet tested at volume |
| **Paced sending** | One message at a time: hourly and daily caps (default 10 and 80; settable up to 30 and 200), quiet hours (default 21:00 to 07:00), random gaps, a typing indicator, a check that the number is on WhatsApp. Pause any time. At 20 per hour a message leaves about every three minutes, so 1,000 messages take days. | Outbox | Verified for real sends at low volume; the full pacing logic is tested with a stand-in |
| **Replies skip the queue** | An approved answer to someone who wrote to you is sent on the next tick, ahead of cold messages, outside the hourly and daily limits and quiet hours, and it does not delay the cold messages. | Outbox | Tested with a stand-in; not yet seen on a real reply |
| **Safety stops** | Three failures in a row pause the account. A message interrupted mid-send is marked failed and never resent automatically. A number that replies STOP (or similar) is blocked for good, its pending messages are cancelled and the lead is closed as lost ("Meminta berhenti (STOP)"). | Outbox | Verified with real STOP replies |
| **Email channel** | The same review and approval flow over your own SMTP account: AI draft plus subject, edit, approve, paced sending with an unsubscribe link and postal address in every message. Addresses come from contact enrichment. Sending is blocked, and approving email drafts is refused, while `PUBLIC_API_URL` is not a public https address, because recipients could not open the unsubscribe link. | Outbox → Email | Verified against a local TLS SMTP server and a few real sends. Replies are not read. See [email-channel.md](email-channel.md) |
| **Reply planning** | The action of a reply is chosen in code from the classified intent: no reply (automatic greeting), a fixed polite wording (thanks and goodbye, "will pass it to my superior", "later", no model and no draft used), or a model-written answer or next step limited to the facts you have and to one or two sentences. The model is told it already introduced itself. A draft that introduces itself again, commands, uses emoji, guesses an honorific or invents a link is rejected, retried once with the reason, and replaced by safe wording flagged "fallback" if it fails again. | Outbox | Tested with real replies; the first real reply drafts since this change have not been reviewed yet |
| **Replies** | A reply from a known lead updates the CRM status, cancels pending follow-ups and creates a reply draft. The draft is written for what the sender is, a business we approached: polite, humble, no commands, no emoji, no guessed honorific, and when they say they will pass it to a superior it thanks them and waits. Unknown numbers are stored, never answered. A shop's automatic greeting ("Terima kasih telah menghubungi…", "ada yang bisa kami bantu?", "mohon menunggu", opening hours, a catalogue with links) is recognised by rules, and so is any first answer that arrives within 45 seconds of our message without a clear question, and changes nothing. | Outbox | Verified with real replies. The rules for automatic greetings come from real examples and will miss some |
| **Hidden-number senders** | WhatsApp sometimes gives only an internal ID (LID) instead of the sender's number. Such messages are stored and listed under "Messages from unknown senders" on the Pipeline page, but cannot be matched to a lead, so a STOP from one must be handled by hand. | Pipeline | Known limit (needs Baileys 7) |
| **Pipeline to closing** | Replies are classified by rules (no AI cost; a model reads only what the rules leave as "other", against the whole conversation), set the stage forward only, mark the lead as waiting for you and stamp a follow-up date when the lead asks for time. Deal value, lost reason and follow-up date are edited on the lead. The Pipeline page lists stage counts and value, waiting replies, quiet leads (7 days), due follow-ups, per-campaign results and lost reasons. Nothing is closed automatically. | Pipeline, Lead detail | Rules covered by unit tests; not yet run against real replies |
| **First-message copy** | First messages open with a hook (a question about a common problem, a fact from the listing, or an everyday outcome), name what the offer fixes in the owner's words, hint at one concrete thing from the brief, and end with a question that invites a reply. Three openings are rotated across a campaign and the one used is stored in the draft's trace so reply rates can be compared. | Outbox | Checked by reading drafts from a local model; reply rates per opening not yet measured |
| **Follow-ups** | Drafts after 3 and 5 days of silence, at most two. Also approved by you. A message you typed on your phone also restarts the wait. | Outbox | Simulated |
| **WhatsApp formatting** | Drafts keep WhatsApp's own formatting (`*bold*`, `_italic_`, `~strike~`, `- ` lists, blank lines between paragraphs): Markdown is converted, unbalanced symbols are removed, bold stays sparing. The Outbox shows each message as the bubble it will be. Email stays plain text. | Outbox | Verified in a browser (rendering); the formatting itself is WhatsApp's |
| **Files and link previews** | Upload a file (PDF, JPG, PNG, WebP, MP4, DOCX, XLSX, PPTX, up to 16 MB; the type is read from the file's own bytes) and send it with the message text as its caption, for single messages and for "One message for many leads". A link in the message gets a preview card (title, description, picture), built through the safe fetcher and shown before you approve; it is on by default when a link is present. A text longer than a caption (1,024 characters) goes out right after the file. A file still used by a waiting message cannot be deleted, and a missing file fails the message instead of sending the text alone. Email is not supported; the official connector refuses files. | Outbox | Simulated end to end against a real database and a real page; uploading and the preview card checked in a browser. Delivery to a real WhatsApp was not tested. |
| **Your own phone messages** | What you type to a lead from your phone or WhatsApp Web is recorded, only for numbers that belong to a lead (any other chat is ignored and never stored). The lead stops waiting for an answer, drafts written for something you already answered are withdrawn, later drafts know what you said, and these messages do not count toward the sending limits. | Outbox, Pipeline | Simulated: the logic against a real database; the real WhatsApp event for messages sent from a phone has not been observed |
| **Reading the whole conversation** | A reply is read together with the bubbles written with it (within 3 minutes of each other), a shop's automatic messages set aside, and what you sent before, including from your phone. Photo and document captions and shared contact cards count. A draft is written a few seconds after their last bubble, replaces any older waiting draft, and is skipped when you have already answered. | Outbox | Simulated, plus unit tests built from real failures |
| **Pointed to another contact** | "Contact our management" or a shared contact card is its own kind of reply: a fixed thank-you chosen in code (or a request for the number), the number kept on the lead, and an "Add as a lead" button that never adds the same number twice. | Outbox, Lead detail | Simulated |
| **Re-read conversations** | On the Pipeline page, a preview then an apply: every conversation is read again as a whole, labels and "waiting" status are corrected, stages only move forward, outdated drafts are withdrawn and a fresh one is written where an answer is still owed. A lead whose only "replies" were a shop's greeting bot goes back to "contacted", a number that asked to stop (even inside a sentence) but was never blocked is blocked, and a specific label is never overwritten with "other". Stages given automatically are corrected from the highest stage a conversation honestly reached (a stage you set by hand is never lowered), polite closings ("Ok kk") are a reply nobody has to answer, and a conversation that went on with answers of yours that were never recorded is left as it is and reported as incomplete. | Pipeline | Simulated (API against a real database); the page itself not yet checked in a browser |

## Safety built in

- **Human approval** before every message.
- **Website reading** refuses private and internal addresses (including names that resolve to them), re-checks every redirect, limits size and time, and runs the browser behind a proxy that resolves each name once and connects to the checked address.
- **Outside text is data** in every AI prompt (web pages, customer replies, scraped listings, exported chats). Drafts are scanned afterwards.
- **No fabricated evidence**: the brief's proof is grounded in the sources, and drafts may only mention proof that is in the brief.
- **Opt-out is absolute** and checked when you approve and again when the message is sent.

## What Prospex is not (yet)

Prospex covers one part of marketing well: **finding local businesses, understanding your offer, and conversations by WhatsApp**. It is not yet a full marketing suite. Not built:

- Official WhatsApp Business Cloud API. Routing, the entitlement check (hosted plans only) and the settings choice exist; the connector is not built. See [editions.md](editions.md).
- Instagram, LinkedIn or SMS sending. Email is built (see above) but does not read replies or send follow-ups; the "reply" lane described above is WhatsApp only; LinkedIn notes are generated for high-priority leads but not sent.
- Content and SEO writing, social posts and calendars, ads, landing pages, reviews and reputation, attribution beyond the CRM counters.
- Teams with several users per workspace (the Settings screen shows the current user only) and workspace deletion. Removing a member takes effect within about 10 seconds (the token's membership is re-checked).
- Proposals and invoices, an agent or MCP server.

The roadmap in the [README](../README.md#roadmap) lists what is next.
